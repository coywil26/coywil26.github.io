description: "Bernardo is a biophysicist specialized in combining experimental, computational and statistical approaches to understand the structure, function, and regulation of ion channels. His work combines electrophysiology, quantitative biophysical modeling and machine learning to investigate the molecular mechanisms underlying ion channel gating and thermodynamics. During his Ph.D. in the laboratory of Dr. Ramon Latorre at the Universidad de Valparaiso, he elucidated the mechanisms of voltage- and calcium-dependent gating in connexin hemichannels. As a PEW Latin American Fellow in the laboratory of Dr. Francisco Bezanilla at the University of Chicago, he developed a novel optical temperature step method to characterize the thermodynamics of voltage-gated potassium channels, leading to discoveries of mutations that alter channel temperature sensitivity. More recently, his research has focused using protein structure prediction and electrophysiology to uncover the gating mechanism of Kv channels leading to the elucidation of the inhibition mechanism of 4-aminopyridine, a drug used to improve nerve conduction in patients with multiple sclerosis. He joined the Coyote-Maestas Lab to develop and apply large-scale experimental approaches for studying ion channel function."
